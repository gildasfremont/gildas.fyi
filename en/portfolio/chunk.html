A marketplace for marble slabs: an unfamiliar market, set out in four equations before the first screen

Chunk, a marketplace for marble slabs, and why it stopped at the inventory tool

Autumn 2020 to 2021 · co-founder and CPO, in a joint venture with Mineral Expertise · my first assignment as an independent

A marble slab is a unique piece: two slabs from the same block do not have the same veining, and a buyer travels to the yard to choose theirs. Mineral Expertise, which sells high-end marble to architects, hotels and yacht builders, offered me to co-found a marketplace for these slabs. I knew nothing of the stone trade, its channels or its buyers.

The thesis, in four equations

Before drawing anything, I wrote down what the project assumed, as a chain of equivalences. It can be said aloud in four sentences, and it explains why the first product to build was not the marketplace.

  • a scan=a slab online
  • slabs online=an inventory online
  • an inventory and prices=a catalogue
  • catalogues=a marketplace
1. InventoryA stock tool plugged into the scanners, useful to a seller before the first buyer arrives.
2. The marketplaceThe inventories brought together, with images that are easy to produce and share.
3. TransactionsPayment, logistics, integration on the sellers' side.

The first six months were fieldwork: yard visits, price lists, trade press, and an order-tracking board built for a factory in Lyon, machine by machine. The field confirmed the problem: around a single slab, the scanner, WhatsApp, the production spreadsheets and the PDF quotes each held part of the information, and none passed it on to the others.

The POC

The data model started from the exports of a scanner on the market, with their three measurements, outer, commercial, inner, and the outline of the slab. A side idea: the sticker placed on the slab already carries alignment marks and colour dots, so a plain photo with the sticker in frame can give the real dimensions and the shade, where a scanner costs twenty to thirty thousand euros.

The stop

A useful marketplace assumes that sellers share their stock and their prices. Their business rests in part on controlling that information.

The field had confirmed the problem and shown how hard the solution was. The stone trade rests on the relationship between a seller and their customer, and making stock and terms visible to others reduces the seller's control over that relationship. Tools for selling slabs already existed without having shifted those habits. I stopped the marketplace track, and the team moved on to the next project in the same industry, Guinet-Derriaz. What the exploration established is not that a stone marketplace is impossible, but that ours depended on a change in commercial behaviour that nothing allowed us to take for granted.
