Selling the next product of an HR suite from one of the customer's own figures, their staff turnover, rather than from the catalogue

A cross-sell journey inside the product: one indicator page, three solutions, one page per solution

February 2025 · research and analysis, mockups · fractional product director at Lucca

Lucca sells a suite one product at a time. A customer comes in through one or two, then adds more over the years, and part of the growth comes from that next product. The question in February 2025: can the software suggest it by itself to the administrator who is already logged in, from what it knows about the company.

7 → 3products lined up under one figure in the first version, solutions in the one kept.

The first version, one page and seven products

The first version fitted on one page. The company's staff turnover at the top, “according to your latest data from Socle RH”, its definition, two national figures, then seven products of the suite one under the other, each with its pitch, a testimonial and a “Discover” link.

Seven answers to a single question: under the figure, the page turned back into a catalogue.

The indicator first, three solutions after

The version kept starts from the same figure, one the customer already has: their staff turnover, computed by the Socle RH product they use. The sector to compare it with, the range considered normal, the definition, the trend, the warning signs. The solutions come after, and there are only three left. Each reads as an outcome, “Ensure a fair and transparent pay policy”, and the product that delivers it is named underneath, in small type.

One page per solution, and the path from one product to the next

Each solution opens its own page. The pay one restates the problem in a line, shows three uses of the product with a capture for each, then three practical cases. Under each case, the customer's path: the products they had, the one they added. The page ends on the roll-out in three moments and on an advisor to contact.

Three rules written in the working file

  • For administrators, within their scope.The indicator comes from the platform's data. The footnote says so: the data is confidential, and only admin-level users see it.
  • An indicator can mislead.In a fast-growing startup, staff turnover is distorted. The page says so and points to a second indicator, the retention rate.
  • By legal entity, rather than global.A single line in the file, next to the mockups.

What we do not know

No measured effect. The assignment was research, and these screens are my working mockups: they come from my Figma drafts, not from Lucca's instance.
