Portfolio · Monbanquet, 2017 to 2019

At an online caterer, putting margin and kitchen load into the quote generator, so that a salesperson no longer promises the impossible

A bespoke quote in thirty minutes instead of several days, at an online caterer that does not cook itself

Product director and first product manager · 2017 to 2019 · reporting to the CEO, with the sales team, operations and the developers

Monbanquet sold corporate buffets prepared by a network of more than seventy independent artisans. A caterer that does not cook itself lives on promises: every order is a bespoke quote, and every quote has to hold its margin while respecting constraints that nobody can see at once. What each artisan can produce that day, the picking, the delivery rounds, the load on each kitchen on that date.

A salesperson writing a quote has no way of knowing which one is impossible. Quotes went out in several days, checked by operations after the fact, reworked or refused, while the customer waited for a menu that the competition, for its part, was sending.

A quote, before and after

Before
  1. The customer makes a request.
  2. The salesperson drafts a menu.
  3. Operations check it, days later.
  4. Rework, or refusal.
  5. The quote goes out.
Several days.
After
  1. The customer makes a request.
  2. The generator only offers menus that operations can deliver that day, at a margin that holds.
  3. The quote goes out.
Thirty minutes.

What the generator knows

The marginThe pickingThe delivery roundsThe load on each kitchen, on that date

The choice was to put the constraints inside the generation, not in a check after the fact. The alternative, a catalogue of fixed menus, gave up what set Monbanquet apart; speeding up the check kept the problem, only faster. The cost: a generator that sometimes says no to salespeople paid on what they sign, and a small team that spent its time modelling kitchen constraints rather than making screens. The hardest part was not the interface but getting the kitchens to say what they knew without ever having written it down.

The website, as a single path

The website showed lists of menus and descriptions of offers. I rebuilt it as a linear funnel, a single path from request to quote, plugged into the same generator. The figure we tracked, the share of visitors who go all the way through, rose by half.

What came of it

30 minfor a bespoke quote, where the competition took several days.
+50%more visitors completing the funnel.
2020Monbanquet is acquired by B2B Food Group, after 10,000 events and 500,000 guests served.

“It's what saved the company.”

The CEO, about the quote generator.

The sales team used it every day, and operations stopped reworking quotes already sent. No mockups from the time were kept: this page rests on the facts, without screens.
